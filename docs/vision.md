## What the proposal establishes

- The site is a durable way to distribute work worth sharing, not merely an online résumé or a demonstration of front-end technique.
- Its technical side is a personal forum for algorithms, reasoning, experiments, and failed approaches that may still teach something. UBQ is the first central subject.
- Its photographic side should preserve and communicate the meaning Nicolas sees in selected photographs, including short context where appropriate.
- It should serve curious first-time visitors, technical learners, expert or returning readers, recruiters, and collaborators without forcing them through the same depth of explanation.
- The experience should be **interesting**, **informative**, and **approachable**. In implementation terms: use visual explanation when it adds understanding, provide a useful fact or next step at every level, and move from plain language toward technical detail through progressive disclosure.
- The site may function as a professional calling card, so About, CV, evidence, and approved contact paths must remain quick to find and easy to scan.

## What the proposal alone does not establish

It does not supply a biography, professional dates, contact details, profile URLs, CV facts, photographs, captions, publication dates, UBQ's expanded name, performance results, Nicolas's exact contribution, or authoritative project-resource links.

Those content facts remain owner decisions. The later creative-direction interview does settle the broad palette, typography, name treatment, homepage posture, interaction principles, article annotations, footnotes, diagram workflow, and photography behavior. Exact color values, dimensions, motion values, and several responsive details remain prototype gates rather than open brand questions.

Coursework language about grading, references to other websites, and rhetorical examples in the proposal are context, not feature requirements or permission to copy another site's identity.

## Current translation into the site

- Visitor-facing pages describe the purpose of the work instead of narrating the implementation scaffold.
- UBQ is organized as **Orient → Use → Inspect → Verify**, ending in real paper, source, documentation, and writing links when those are supplied.
- Writing can include design reasoning and failed paths; unfinished topic titles remain visibly editorial and are never presented as published articles.
- Photography is framed around selected images plus remembered context; no substitute imagery is fabricated.
- The approved identity uses absolute black, near-white Times New Roman, a plain lowercase `nebve.com` name treatment, and lime interaction states. The homepage is an index rather than a hero.
- Markdown-compatible authored files should become the content source of truth, with ordinary Markdown as the default and native Svelte components available when a piece genuinely needs custom logic.
- The current cream/green interface, card system, grid decoration, large hero, and `N` favicon are provisional implementation artifacts, not approved identity choices.
- Unsupported content and unresolved prototype values stay clearly outside the launch-ready surface.
